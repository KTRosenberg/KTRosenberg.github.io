Course Description

A cross-disciplinary course that brings students together to design and prototype their own original interactive experience projects within 2D games and animations, creative user interfaces, or applications of emerging technologies. The course especially welcomes students from different backgrounds who are excited to learn “how things work” and combine technology, art, and design. Students will explore interactive systems and game programming to reach a fine-level of understanding at the coding level through a mix of guided practice and open-ended projects. For personal projects, they will be taken through the prototyping process from initial ideas to a working portfolio piece. For scaffolding, motivation, and inspiration, students will also be introduced to examples in human-computer interface design, games, and applications of human-computer interaction (HCI). HCI can help us understand how to design interactive systems from the perspective of human-centered needs and experiences. This includes techniques such as storyboarding, playtests, and human-centered user research. Through the course, students will take-away: transferable design- and problem-solving skills beyond coding; collaborative project experience; practice designing and creating real-time systems; appreciation for ideas in HCI to assist in the creative process; their own creative portfolio pieces.

General Topics for the Semester (with Room for Improvisation)

  • Learning about each other's interests
  • Idea formation and ideation techniques, brainstorming, sketching, storyboarding
  • Game programming techniques throughout, with some things learned from core courses re-applied for interactive systems and others completely new. Topics may include sprite animation, animation and physics loops, project structure, collision detection, 2D graphics programming. There is room for flexibility based on interests.
  • Game design techniques throughout, e.g., thinking about the feel and mechanics of the game.
  • Case studies throughout, like looking at example games or the development process or programming of existing games to think about how they work, whenever possible.
  • Idea recording and presentation throughout: students will log their creative process and creative choices. There will be structured check-points in the form of project proposals for discussion with the professor. There will be a presentation component. Things will be scaffolded so students will have creative freedom without getting lost. Experimentation and course correction are actually a natural part of the process too!

Questions and Answers

  • Q: "Are there exams?"
    A: No, this is a projects course, but it requires commitment and self-motivation. Think of it like a small capstone or thesis course that gives you the chance to focus on something you'd really love to do/learn. This course will be about going through your own creative process on a project focused on prototyping your own game idea. You'll also not just be evaluated on the outcome of the project. You will have a journal, presentations, discussions, and many opportunities to demonstrate what you've learned.
  • Q: "What tools and engines will we use?"
    A: We won't use a AAA engine like Unity or Unreal because I want this course to be more about learning some of the fundamentals from scratch. The goal is also to make a prototype instead of a polished product, so there's no need to feel obligated to make something perfect. TBA, we'll use a provided library built for the class that should be powerful enough to give you freedom of expression while being simple enough to inspect if you were to go into the files.
  • Q: "May I use generative AI?"
    A: Students in this course ideally shouldn't want to use AI. The course is about giving students the chance to go through an entire creative process and get the fulfillment from it as artists. Respectfully, regardless of whatever attitude someone has about AI, this is specifically for those who want the full experience of making something on their own that they understand fully. I would ask what you want out of the course if you're asking this question. Ideally, I'd love to work with students who want to treat this like a passion project and art class.
  • Q: (Any concern about C or C++)
    A: these languages are used a lot. Yes, if used in complicated ways, they can be... complicated. However, they can also feel simple if we focus on the basics. We won't need all of the features, and as long as you have the fundamentals, it should be straightforward to get the hang of the additional techniques we'll cover. There is plenty of open-source tooling and libraries for C++. These languages also can be built for the web, so if you're more familiar with creative coding in JS, it may be useful to know some of these languages as well.
  • Q: "I have a question..."
    A: e-mail me and I can answer.
